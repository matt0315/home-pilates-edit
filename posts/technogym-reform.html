Technogym Reform at $18,550: who that price is actually for

$18,550 AUD

Price as seen on 25 November 2026 (Australia/Perth) on Technogym’s Australian site: $18,550, listed as inclusive of delivery and professional installation. The page also said delivery is typically scheduled within two weeks of order confirmation.

Technogym Reform is a pilates reformer in the same way a very expensive kitchen is a place to cook: it does the job, and a large part of what you are buying is design, installation and a brand that already sits in high-end gyms. The Australian site listed it at $18,550 on the day we checked, inclusive of delivery and professional installation, with a call to schedule delivery, typically within two weeks of confirmation, plus onboarding from Technogym staff.

The published picture is of a contemporary machine with an ergonomic backrest, a three-position headrest, two-position shoulder rests, high-quality springs, a quick-tilting footbar with four positions, and a sliding mechanism with nine configurations. Dimensions are 246 cm long and 89 cm wide, at 115 kg. Handles, foot straps and a grip pad are included; a box and a platform extender are optional extras. There is also the Technogym App, with guided sessions, which is a large part of the “usable at home” argument at this price.

Who it suits

Studios, retreats, and private homes that already spend at this level on equipment, and that want installation, a quiet carriage, and a machine that looks like furniture rather than gym stock. It is not a first home reformer, and it is not a sensible “upgrade” from a $1,400 folding wooden bed unless that extra money is genuinely available and the space can take a 246 by 89 cm footprint that does not fold away in the same way a compact home model does.

Pros

  • The price includes delivery and professional installation, which is not a small line item on a 115 kg machine.
  • Clear adjustability (headrest, shoulder rests, tilting and sliding footbar) and guided app sessions, aimed at more than one skill level.
  • A design-led finish, with colour options such as Sand Stone and Pearl Grey, if the machine will live in a visible room.

Cons

  • $18,550 is many times the price of a competent folding home reformer. The on-paper exercise is not eighteen times better, so the extra money has to be for design, service and longevity, not for “doing pilates”.
  • The footprint is large, and optional extras such as the box are not in the base price. Measure the room, including clearance to use the straps.
  • We have not used this machine. The illustration is original artwork of a generic contemporary reformer, with no Technogym logo, and should not be taken as a photograph of the product.

If you are outfitting a serious space and the installation package matters, this is the premium end of the Australian market and it should be bought after a proper conversation with the retailer, not from a link-in-bio note. If you want a folding machine for a spare room, look at Your Reformer or Lifespan instead. The useful comparison is not “is Technogym good?” — it is “is this the right kind of purchase at all?” For most home practitioners, it is not.
